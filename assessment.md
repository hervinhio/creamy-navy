This is a **much more complete homepage now**. The biggest change is that you've moved from designing a hero to designing an actual **shopping journey**.

And importantly, the page now has a clear narrative:

**Rentrée → ways to buy → categories → products → trousseau service**

That is a strong structure.

I would now put the homepage around **8.5/10 visually/structurally**, with the remaining work being mostly about polish, product credibility, conversion details, and consistency.

## What has improved most

The new page answers the four questions a visitor has:

1. **What is this?** → School supplies / rentrée
2. **What can I do?** → Buy online, boutique, or send a trousseau
3. **What do you sell?** → Categories + products
4. **Why should I care?** → You can prepare the whole school list for me

That's a significant improvement over the first version.

# 5. The category cards could be slightly more premium

Right now they feel a little like six independent UI cards.

I'd make them feel more like a **single category system**.

For example:

* same image/icon area height
* identical typography positioning
* identical CTA positioning
* consistent icon stroke width
* consistent hover behavior

And I'd increase the visual size of the actual icon slightly.

The "Découvrir" links are quite small.

I'd make the entire card clickable, not just the text.

So the interaction becomes:

> **click anywhere on the card → category**

rather than forcing users to hit "Découvrir".

# 12. But there's a contradiction between the trousseau section and the hero

The bottom says:

> **Récupérez votre commande prête en boutique.**

So the hero makes the service sound potentially like:

while the detailed section specifically says:

The more important rule here is:

**Don't make the promise bigger in the hero than the service actually is.**

At this point, I wouldn't make another major redesign.

Use **real school products** and real product names/prices.

### Priority 2 — Make product cards genuinely shoppable

Add **Ajouter au panier** / quick actions.

### Priority 3 — Add trust

Testimonials, school partners, real customer statistics, or other verifiable credibility signals.

Especially for first-time customers.

Make the pickup/delivery promise consistent everywhere.

Use the same category names in navigation and category cards.

# One particularly important observation

You're now very close to having a **distinctive Ma rentrée design language**.

I can summarize what you've created as:

> **Paper + school + warmth + commerce + hand-drawn personality**

That's a much more ownable identity than a generic blue education website.

The key now is **not to make everything look hand-drawn**.

Instead:

> **Let the brand be playful. Let the commerce be precise.**

That's the principle I'd use for the rest of the website.

Your homepage is now doing a good job of making the visitor understand the business. The next thing that will determine whether it feels like a polished commercial product is **the quality and consistency of the actual catalogue, product cards, checkout, trust signals, and mobile experience**. Those are where I'd spend the next design effort rather than changing this hero again.
